Welcome to the September issue! I hope it does a little to ease all that ails you and maybe the world's ails too, if we're lucky. Keep reading, keep creating, my friends! 

Our gorgeous cover art this issue was created by Veronica O'Neill. Read on to learn more about her creative process and inspirations in our interview below.

**Tell us a bit about yourself and what got you on the path to being an illustrator. Was there a defining moment that made you decide to make art your career?**

I’m Veronica - a fantasy illustrator from Australia!  I’ve been working professionally for 6 years but before then, I was one of those kids who couldn’t stop drawing on any piece of paper lying around. My interest in pursuing art as a career actually came from my love of gaming and specifically the concept art books for the Assassin Creed games. Those books were the first time that I realised it was possible to have a career as an artist. My first few professional jobs were for video game concept art before I discovered that I preferred painting big illustrations.
<img src="https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/645ad6fd045c834f9c300cf4/7b3c56af-a6cc-4866-ba48-34aaad294aba/Veronica+ONeill+-+Goddess+of+the+River+Endpaper.jpg?format=1000w" class="lg:ml-5 lg:float-right lg:w-1/2" />

The next few years led me to shift to working freelance as an illustrator, starting out in TTRPGs before moving more recently into book art. I absolutely love that I get to paint art for a living and share my love of fantasy with folks worldwide.


**The cover image has got such a powerful quality to it. What was the inspiration for it?**

Thank you! It’s certainly a piece that’s captured a lot of interest since I painted it. The original inspiration for the piece was an old artwork of mine that depicted a soldier kneeling in front of a dryad queen. I never quite managed to nail the idea I had in mind and so left it partially unfinished. Then in 2023, I remembered the original painting and took the core concept of the dryad queen, as well as the colour palette, into this new portrait.

I specifically wanted to paint a version of a dryad that was more forest than person. She’s deadly and you can’t assume you know what she is thinking just because she looks vaguely human.

**Who are your favorite women illustrators, past or present?**
<img src="https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/645ad6fd045c834f9c300cf4/96b06fee-43dc-49ee-96e4-1b55db968e15/Valjean+-+Veronica+ONeill.png?format=500w" class="lg:mr-5 lg:float-left lg:w-1/2" />

There are so many incredible women illustrators who I’ve admired and were huge inspirations for me in starting my career in illustration. In recent times, I’ve particularly enjoyed works from Rovina Cai, April Prime, Julie Dillon, Eleanor Piteira and Erion Makuo, to name a few. Each of them have such distinct styles and ways of handling colour that I just adore. I have met both Rovina and April previously, who were very sweet when I rambled about my art ambitions. On my desk I have original sketch cards from the two of them which are great inspirations while I paint.

I also had the chance to learn under a mentorship from the incredible Cynthia Sheppard at the end of last year, whose kind words and advice were instrumental in helping me navigate this next part of my art journey.

**What is your dream project? If there were no restrictions on time or money what would you create?**

I would absolutely love to work on some of my favourite fantasy novels, so hopefully one day I get that opportunity. Outside of that, if I was given the time and funding to do something personal for myself then I’d love to work on one of two things -  Either a series of worldbuilding concepts and artworks based on some personal stories I’ve written over the years, or alternatively a huge set of dragon illustrations (I just adore painting them so much).
<img src="https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/645ad6fd045c834f9c300cf4/18df1330-88d3-44d1-9a2b-c671820fb372/Draegoth+Veronica+ONeill.png?format=500w" class="lg:ml-5 lg:float-right lg:w-1/2" />

**Because LSQ is all about stories I have to ask, what are some of your favorite tales?**

There have been plenty that have inspired me over the years. As a kid I was absolutely star struck by Chris D’Lacey’s “Fire Within” chronicles which prominently featured the most amazing dragons and is where my username “zookie” comes from on my social media platforms. My teen years were also hugely influenced by Terry Brooks and his Shannara novels.

These days my tastes are still firmly focused on the fantasy genre but there are a few books outside that genre, such as Les Misérables, that I just adore too. I love a good epic story and especially one that centres on very human struggles.

A recent story that really stood out would be “The Word for World is Forest” by Ursula K. Le Guin. A very hard hitting novel that I had to sit with for a long while after finishing it.

**What's something you're working on now that you're really excited about?**

There’s plenty that I can’t say unfortunately because of NDAs, but I’m working on two particular projects right now that are both very exciting for me as they involve two clients that I’ve wanted to work with for a long time now. One of the projects involves painting some fantastical creatures which is always extra fun!

**Where can people learn more about you and your work?**

My current most active social media profile is on instagram under @zookie_art, however I also do share my work over on bsky (zookie-art) and threads too. I love to share little snippets about my life, including my time birding while I travel around for events, and also WIPs of upcoming art.

For anyone interested in learning a bit more about pursuing art as a career, my DMs/email are always available too!
